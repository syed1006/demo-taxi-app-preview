Famous Temples Near Bangalore Worth a Day Trip by Cab

Updated 29 July 2026 2 min read By the Bangalore Urban Cabs driver team

Some of South India’s most remarkable sacred architecture sits within a half-day’s drive of Bangalore — from a pillar that famously doesn’t touch the ground to a Shiva linga taller than a ten-storey building. Here’s the honest shortlist, ordered by distance.

Sri Bhoga Nandeeshwara — 60 km, the time machine

At the foot of Nandi Hills stands one of Karnataka’s oldest living temple complexes — begun in the 9th century and expanded by every dynasty that followed: Ganga, Chola, Hoysala, Vijayanagara. The carved kalyani (stepped tank) is among the most photographed in the state, and weekday mornings are blissfully quiet. Pairs naturally with a Nandi Hills sunrise.

Adiyogi, Chikkaballapur — 65 km, the modern icon

The 112-ft Adiyogi at Sadhguru Sannidhi isn’t ancient, but it has become the region’s most visited spiritual landmark. Time it for the evening Divya Darshanam light show if you can stay late — the Nandi Hills + Adiyogi package (₹3,800) covers both with tolls and parking included.

Kotilingeshwara, Kolar — 100 km, the surreal one

A 33-metre (108-ft) Shiva linga — among the tallest anywhere — watched by a 35-ft Nandi and surrounded by lakhs of smaller lingas installed by devotees, stretching in painted rows to the horizon. Devotees can still sponsor and install a linga in their own name. The Kotilingeshwara 1-Day Package (₹4,000) makes it an easy, restful day.

Lepakshi — 120 km, the masterpiece

The 16th-century Veerabhadra temple is peak Vijayanagara: India’s best-surviving ceiling frescoes, a monolithic Nandi the size of a bus, the giant Jatayu of Ramayana legend nearby — and the famous hanging pillar, which rests clear of the floor (guides will slide a cloth beneath it to prove the point). Just across the Andhra border, so carry photo ID. The Lepakshi combo day (₹5,800) strings it with Nandi Hills and the Adiyogi.

Tirupati — 250 km, the pilgrimage

The big one — Sri Venkateswara at Tirumala draws more pilgrims than almost any shrine on Earth, and it rewards planning over spontaneity: darshan slots, dress codes, the 22-km ghat climb. Read the full Tirupati guide first, then take the unhurried 2-day package (₹8,600) — or ask us to pace a one-day dash around your darshan slot.

Don’t overlook the city itself

Visiting family with only an afternoon free? The Bangalore City Darshan (₹3,800) covers ISKCON, the Bull Temple and Tipu’s mosque-adjacent old town alongside the palaces — all inside city limits.

Quick answers

Which temple trip is best for a single free day?

The Lepakshi + Nandi Hills + Adiyogi combo covers the most ground — a Vijayanagara masterpiece, a 9th-century Dravidian complex and the 112-ft Adiyogi in one 6 AM to 8 PM loop.

Do any of these need special planning?

Tirupati does — book your Special Entry darshan slot on the TTD portal before fixing the travel date. Everything else is walk-in; just start early to beat heat and crowds.

Are dress codes strict?

Traditional or modest dress is expected everywhere and mandatory at Tirupati's Special Entry darshan. Shoes come off at every temple — slip-ons make the day easier.

Is the driver bata and parking included in package prices?

On our 1-day temple packages, yes — driver bata, tolls and parking are in the fixed price. Entry fees, seva bookings and offerings stay yours.
